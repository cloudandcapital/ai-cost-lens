A GUIDE TO THE TOOL

Understand your AI costs.
Start with what you have.

Use a bill to understand spending, or compare two AI options using their cost and a small sample of your work. You can begin without connecting an account or preparing a spreadsheet.

Testing a model, prompt, effort, caching, or batch-processing change? Jump to the Verify walkthrough.

Choose your starting point.

I have a bill or subscription charge.

Choose Start a review → Start with a bill. Enter the amount, currency, invoice date, and service period if you know it. A previous bill or same-period budget adds context. Without usage records, this establishes a cost baseline; it cannot tell you which work caused the bill.

I pay for several AI tools.

Choose Review my AI tools. Enter subscription charges and separately billed usage for one period. Include a source for each charge and an owner when you know who is responsible. Check for duplicate charges and avoid adding usage already included in a subscription.

I want to compare two options.

Choose Compare two options. The form starts blank. Enter their monthly costs, how many tasks you complete, and the usable outputs and review time from a sample of the same work. The result estimates cost per usable result and shows the monthly value you assign to reviewing and fixing time.

In Review my AI tools, explicitly choose whether the amounts are checked bills, estimates, or allocated costs. Confirm that the charges share one period and do not overlap. Neither choice independently verifies a provider bill.

I have usage records, or want to price a prompt.

Review AI usage analyzes supported request or trace exports. Upload what you have checks supported CSV, JSON, and text-based invoice PDFs. A scanned PDF may need manual entry. Price a prompt models API costs using the tool's dated price catalog and your assumptions; it does not run your prompt or fetch live prices.

A cheaper plan can mean more work.

This invented example compares 100 tasks per month. You check 20 outputs from each plan and value your time at $30 an hour.

The calculation projects sample review time and usable-output rates across the monthly task count. Time value is an estimate, not necessarily cash paid or money you can recover.

Read the result in two parts.

  1. What the numbers say. Compare the costs on the same basis. In the simple comparison, “usable” means accepted without a significant fix. Repaired outputs are excluded from this first-pass measure.
  2. What still needs checking. A lower estimate does not establish quality, permission to use a tool, or realized savings. Keep the quality and approval checks separate from the cost finding.

Approval stays visible in the result as your own confirmation, separate from the cost finding. If you rename a tool, its approval is cleared and must be confirmed again. Use Edit this comparison to change a comparison, or Edit these charges to update a combined-tool baseline. Changed charges or service-period details require checking the charge boundary again. Start a review begins a fresh draft. Older saved comparisons remain readable; their original editable inputs may not be available.

Test one API change before adopting it.

Verify is part of the detailed cost-per-ready-result review. It is not shown inside bill baselines, combined-tool baselines, or the simple two-option comparison. Download any open comparison before replacing it. For a walkthrough, choose Explore an example on the welcome screen, then Verify in the navigation across the top, between Simulate and Evidence. On a small screen, use the section dropdown. Choose Explore a caching example to see a completed test with invented data.

To test your own setup, use the form headed Did this change earn its place? The tool records the results you supply; it does not run either setup or call a provider for you.

  1. Lock the starting point. Name the workload, define what makes a result ready, and enter the currency. Record before and after values for one change to model, effort, prompt version, caching, or processing mode, along with other settings you know. Confirm the others stay the same and lock your quality thresholds.
  2. Keep validation cases separate. Reserve cases before tuning either setup. Choose tuning or reserved validation when recording a run, and keep the same case IDs and checks within each set. Further tuning after validation starts needs a new test record.
  3. Check both setups on the same work. Use the paired-output review below the test form, or select its saved verification JSON. Each case needs checks and a human outcome for both setups. Record review or correction minutes when available.
  4. Add the matching API costs. Include every call and retry needed for those cases, enter a cost-source reference, and confirm this was a fresh execution of the locked setups. Repeat with fresh outputs and costs. Re-saving a file with different line endings or removing its source hash does not establish a new execution. Matching parsed checks, outcomes and review minutes require a separate confirmation that you actually ran both locked setups again. That declaration is recorded; the tool cannot independently verify execution or detect the same cases renamed with new IDs.

Read quality and cost together.

Each run shows ready-result rates, check results, review minutes when supplied, and API cost per ready result. Forty cases tested twice remain 40 distinct cases, with 80 paired observations across repeats.

A validation run that fails your locked quality rule or does not lower API cost per ready result returns Keep the starting setup. At least two fresh validation runs on the same 30 or more reserved cases, with a declared random or systematic sample, blind review, randomized order, preserved quality, and lower API unit cost in every run can return Candidate worth a finance review. These are minimum workflow checks, not statistical proof or permission to adopt the change. A changed setup with zero ready results is rejected when the starting setup has some, even if a 0% ready floor was entered. A zero floor stays visibly flagged. If the starting setup has no ready results, the tool explains why its unit cost cannot be compared. The paired-output check below has its own thresholds; its pass can differ from the controlled test’s locked quality or cost result.

The caching walkthrough is entirely illustrative. Its first run uses invented API costs of $0.40 before and $0.22 after; its second uses $0.42 before and $0.23 after. Both reuse the same 40 invented cases. The example demonstrates the workflow; it is not a measured provider experiment.

Save the test and carry its evidence forward.

Choose Download test record before leaving the page. Use Open saved test in Verify to reopen it; the tool rebuilds its calculations. If a test is already open, download it if needed and use the confirmation under Start another test before replacing it. The invented caching example is read-only; start a separate test for your own runs. New downloads include an edit check. Reopening an edited file or one without that check shows a notice. This detects casual edits, not authenticity: neither a matching check nor the file’s example label proves where its evidence came from. Print test record creates a separate printable handoff. Raw outputs are excluded from the saved test, but case IDs, rules, settings, and source references remain. Required terms and exact labels may contain the checked answer itself, so keep secrets and sensitive answer text out of those fields.

Attach summary to this review explicitly adds a separate test to the finance memo when workload name, currency, and illustrative status match. Test setup names are not borrowed from the review’s route labels. The memo names the tested change and setups and says that it does not validate the review’s proposed route. The workspace archive keeps the separate test record and an explicit attached/not-attached marker, including when a test could not be attached. Test costs cover API calls only; human work, full operating costs, and production outcomes still need their own evidence. The test does not fill production Actuals or approve savings.

Investigate caching with your usage records.

When a request-log finding flags repeated prefixes with no cache reuse, open What to check before changing caching. It shows the affected requests and available cache-write evidence, then suggests checks for prefix changes, timing, model boundaries, and charges. Those are possible explanations to investigate; a low cache-read count alone does not establish the cause.

Keep a copy you can return to.

Use Download decision record (JSON) to save a comparison. Bill and combined-tool results also offer Save this baseline; it saves the same reopenable review format. Then use Open saved review to reopen it. Print finance memo opens your browser's print dialog so you can print or save a PDF. The tool does not automatically save your review between visits.

Source files are read in your browser. No provider account connection or API key is needed. Exported files contain the information in your review, so store and share them deliberately.
